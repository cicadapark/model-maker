# What 0.2.0's sealed run got wrong, and why (11 November 2026)

*The work these notes planned was called 0.3.0; it was released as 0.2.1 (its results: `docs/results.md`).*

The 25 sealed prompts were made once at the frozen code on each card (the 16 GB and the 12 GB card, the same seeds).
By eye, eight objects came out wrong or flawed while their checks passed or failed for other reasons: a teapot with
large black patches, a glass bottle dark on one side, a flower pot and a bucket smeared inside, a mace made as a long
rod, and three wheels and a club that the picture model did not paint as asked. This note finds each root cause from
the in-between pictures (the six painted views, the bake, the touch-up's fill), names the established technique for
it, and measures what can be measured cheaply on the trials already made. The 25 sealed prompts are development data
now; every measure below is also taken on the other trials (209 finished objects, 167 of them with their paint data).

## How each colour was traced

`painted.npz` keeps the colour cloud: every painted pixel of the six views on the surface, then the touch-up's fill.
The six views come in order (front, the two sides, back, from above, from below), each in rows from the top, so a
side view's points never rise in height: the cloud splits into its views where the height jumps up (and the view from
above and the one from below by their facing). Each object was then baked as the finish bakes it, and baked again
with each painted point's colour replaced by its source's marker colour, and with the corners' own colours (what the
bake uses where no painted point is near) marked as a view's colour seen at a slant (a view that saw the corner at
between 0.2 and 0.55 facing) or spread from neighbours (no view saw it). Rendered from the same turns, each pixel of
the object shows where its colour came from.

## The teapot: one painted view came back black

* **Which stage.** The view painter (MV-Adapter) painted the second view (the side, 90 degrees round) with a black
  body; the front, back and other side are white (picture 02). The bake took it as it was: the black on the finished
  model is that view's paint (picture 03, the cyan marker). The touch-up painted only one view's handle, and the spread
  is under 1 % of the surface. So it is the view, not the bake's choice, the touch-up or the spread.
* **What the bake added.** Where the black view and a white one both see the surface (round 45 degrees), the bake mixes
  each texel's four nearest painted points by distance alone, from whichever views they came: the pixels of the two
  views interleave, and the band shows as a fine black-and-white stipple (picture 04, top). Keeping each painted point
  only where its own view sees the surface most squarely, as texturing from photographs selects a view per place
  (Lempitsky and Ivanov 2007; Waechter, Moehrle and Goesele 2014) and as MV-Adapter's own projection weights views by
  the cosine of their angle to the surface (`ExponentialBlend`, `uv_exp_blend_alpha` 3, in its `pipeline_texture.py`),
  turns the stipple into a clean edge (picture 04, bottom). It does not remove the black: the view itself is wrong.
* **Why no check caught it.** The coverage counts a texel painted whatever its colour. "Painted views not black" counts
  near-black pixels (all channels under 0.04) in the whole frame, backdrop included: the black view's glossy body has
  highlights, and it came to 6.5 % against a limit of 25 % (the check was made for a view that comes back wholly
  black). The likeness ("measured, not checked") compares the model with the picture from the picture's own view,
  which sees that side only at a grazing angle: mean colour difference 8.4, 86 % within 10, better than 143 of the
  187 finished objects that have it (the median is 12.2). Its picture did show the band in red (picture 05), but a
  mean over the whole object cannot see a band.

## The glass bottle: the same cause, not the glass

The second view painted the bottle dark grey to black; the other three side views and the picture show it clear and
pale (picture 06). Of the model's dark pixels, 73 % come from that view (picture 07). An opaque texture has one colour
per place whatever the viewing direction, so a darker look from one side is not glass seen differently: it is one
view out of family with the other three and with the picture. It is the teapot's cause.

## The flower pot and the bucket: not the same thing

The pot and the bucket failed their checks (paint coverage and the fill; the fill), so the checks did their work.
What made their smears differs:

* **The flower pot: the view from above, used at a slant.** 80 % of the grey pixels on the model (low in colour where
  the pot's own colour is strong) are corners' colours from a view that saw them at a slant: the view from above
  painted the inside wall a cold grey (picture 09, the fifth view), and the inside wall faces that view at less than
  0.55, so no painted point of it is kept for the bake, and the bake falls back to the corners' own colours, which took
  that view's grey (the view painter keeps a view's colour for a corner from a facing of 0.2). 14 % is spread from
  neighbours and 4 % the touch-up's fill (picture 08: magenta, red and blue). The touch-up painted the inside, but most
  of its fill there was left out as foreign to the paint round it, so the place shows the slanted grey.
* **The bucket: the touch-up's fill, then the spread.** Of the dark and grey pixels inside it, those not from the side
  views (which cannot see inside), about 60 % are the touch-up's kept fill and 30 % the spread; the view from above
  under 10 % (picture 10). The inpainter painted a shadow at the foot of the inner wall and dark marks where the
  views from above had painted holes (picture 11); dark brown is found in the wood round it, so the fill was kept.

Both are lighting and occlusion painted into the colours: the views and the inpainter paint the inside of a vessel as
it looks in a photograph, darker and greyer than its outside, not its material's own colour. The established answer is
a texture generated without lighting (Paint3D, Zeng et al. 2024, trains its texture model to give lighting-less
textures for this reason; its code is Apache-2.0). No such model is in the registry yet, and whether its weights may
be used is still to be checked.

## The mace, the club and the wheels: the picture model's words

**What the picture model painted.** Every try of the short iron mace on both cards (4 a card) is a long thin rod with a
small knob or finial: a sceptre or a staff, never a short handle under a heavy head (picture 16). The spinning wheel's
four tries are each a spoked wheel on a small stand, with no bench, treadle or spindle; the water wheel's are spoked
wheels, two with a few cogs or slats on the rim, one a drum, none a mill wheel's ring of paddles. The club's eight tries
(two rounds) are canes and plain sticks (picture 17). The picture model knows these things' names only loosely, and the
words gave it nothing else: for a head on a haft they say "standing upright, its head at the top, seen straight from
the side, the whole length in view", which asks for length, and they describe a head only for the war hammer family.

**What the picture judge said.** "Shows the thing asked for" passed the rods as maces: shares of 0.85, 0.72, 0.94 and
0.81 on the 16 GB card and 0.78, 0.54, 0.89, 0.78 on the 12 GB card, the likeliest other "a staff" or "a spear" (the
check passes from 0.75; three of four passed on each card). "mace" is in the kinds, with "a mace, a heavy spiked or
flanged head on a short handle"; the contrast has "a staff, a long plain wooden pole" and "a spear", and no kind that
fits an iron rod with a knob, so the mace's description, which at least names a head on a handle, wins. The judge
passed the wheels without doubt (0.98 to 1.00, the likeliest other a barrel or a coin): it judges whether the picture
is more a spinning wheel than any other kind it knows, and no other kind it knows is a wheel. The club's eight tries
were refused (0.00 to 0.68, the likeliest other "a staff"): rightly, they are sticks. Note that the club shares the
mace's row and so its description, "a heavy spiked or flanged head".

**Changes to the judge, measured on every hafted picture of the trials** (192 pictures, `tools/trial/judge_hafted.py`):

* *A sceptre among the kinds* ("a sceptre, a long thin ornate rod with a small knob at its top") refuses all eight
  rods (0.00 to 0.10), but also all eight tries of "a wooden quarterstaff" (0.06 to 0.69, from 0.65 to 0.98): a staff
  with a turned end is a sceptre to it. Not kept.
* *The club its own kind* ("a club, a short thick wooden cudgel, much heavier at its top"): the club's sticks score
  higher (the best 0.68 to 0.78, past the limit). Not kept: it would pass a stick.
* *"short" as a quality*, a pair like "curved" ("a short {noun}, about as long as a forearm" against "a long {noun},
  as long as a person is tall", and "a short stubby {noun}, its head big and heavy for its short handle" against "a
  long thin {noun}, a small head on the end of a long pole"): the rods score 0.44 to 0.49, so they would be refused.
  But so is nearly every short thing painted upright alone: a blacksmith's hammer 0.10 to 0.70, a war hammer 0.10 to
  0.32, daggers 0.05 to 0.59. A thing alone on a backdrop has no scale; "short" can only be read from proportions, and
  the pair reads them poorly. On the mace's own pictures it does separate (below: 27 of the 29 rods refused, all 26
  maces of the new words passed, 0.98 and over), because its second wording is about a head on a handle; but a short
  sword, the commonest "short" thing, has no head (16 tries of "a curved short sword": 0.02 to 0.83). So "short" would
  have caught the sealed run's mace (its 8 tries scored 0.44 to 0.49, and the job would have ended "no picture showed
  it" instead of making a sceptre), but as a quality of every prompt it would refuse right pictures. Not added: a
  pair for headed weapons only would need its own labelled set, and with the new words below the rods are gone.

**The fix: describe the thing.** The established answer to a model that knows a name only loosely is to describe the
visible details that set the thing apart (Black Forest Labs' prompting guide), which is what the words already do for
the war hammer's head. New words, said after the user's (`words.HEAD_PICTURE_WORDS`, and `THING_PICTURE_WORDS` for a
thing with no kind of its own):

* a mace: "a short one-handed weapon, its heavy head a ball ringed with flanges or spikes, much wider than its handle,
  the handle short, about as long as a forearm";
* a club: "a short thick cudgel, much thicker and heavier at its top than at its grip, about as long as an arm";
* a spinning wheel: "a machine for spinning yarn: a long sloping bench on three legs, a large spoked wheel at one end
  turned by a foot treadle below it, a small spindle and bobbin at the other end";
* a water wheel: "the big wheel of a water mill: its rim lined all round with many flat wooden paddles standing out
  from it, on a thick axle".

**Measured** (`2026-11-11-030-words`, on the 16 GB card, pictures only, `tools/trial/paint_tries.py`): eight tries of
each prompt with the old words and with the new, the pipeline's own seeds for each wording; the four sealed prompts
and seven others not in any earlier run ("an iron mace", "a flanged mace", "a spiked mace", "a heavy wooden club", "a
knotted oak club", "an old spinning wheel", "a mill water wheel"). All 176 tries were labelled by eye from blind sheets
(`tools/trial/blind_sheets.py`: each cut-out alone, shuffled within its kind, under a code; picture 22), the labels
written before the key was opened (the new tries of the four sealed prompts had been seen once before labelling).

| Prompts (tries a wording) | Old words | New words |
|---|---|---|
| maces: short iron, iron, flanged, spiked (32) | 0 maces: 29 rods with a knob, 3 other | **26 maces** (12 upright, 14 lying, tilted or head down), 1 rod, 5 other (two heads, a head mid-shaft) |
| clubs: wooden, heavy wooden, knotted oak (24) | 0 clubs: 19 sticks or canes, 5 other | **13 clubs**, 10 sticks, 1 other |
| spinning wheels: wooden, old (16) | 0: 15 wheels on a stand, 1 unclear | **16 spinning wheels** |
| water wheels: wooden, mill (16) | 4 paddled, 7 plain or helm-like, 4 with cogs, 1 other | **11 paddled**, 3 plain, 2 with cogs |

By prompt, new against old: short iron mace 6 maces of 8 against 0; iron mace 8 against 0; flanged 6 against 0;
spiked 6 against 0; wooden club 4 against 0; heavy wooden club 2 against 0; knotted oak club 7 against 0; each
spinning wheel 8 against 0; wooden water wheel 5 paddled against 4; mill water wheel 6 against 0.

The prompts held out of the measure (the seven new ones) moved as the sealed ones did. Two limits remain. Fourteen of
the 26 maces are not upright: the description comes before the view in the words, and a heavy head is often painted
lying; the shape stage expects a hafted thing upright, so those tries should lose the choice, but that is not measured
here. And the club is still often a stick (10 of 24): a club is a looser idea than a mace.

**The judge on the new pictures** (SigLIP 2 on the card, as the pipeline judges a try): every new mace passes (26 of
26, 1.00), but so do the five "other" maces (two heads, a head mid-shaft) and the one rod: with the new words the
judge does not have to tell them apart any more, because the picture model now paints maces; it could not before (13
of the 29 old rods passed). The clubs: 10 of the 13 new clubs pass, and 6 of the 10 new sticks (the likeliest others
a torch and a staff), against 5 of 19 old sticks. Every spinning wheel passes, old and new; the water wheels pass 16
of 16 with the new words and 11 of 16 with the old. The judge was not changed.

## A new check: does the paint match the picture where the picture shows it?

The idea: draw the painted object from the picture's view (as the likeness does), compare it with the picture region
by region rather than as one mean, and fail a large place that is unlike the picture, so a black patch on a white
teapot fails. Measured on every finished object of every trial (`tools/trial/local_likeness.py`, from each object's
likeness sheet: 187 objects, 153 after copies of the same files are counted once):

* **How.** Both images blurred before the colours are compared, as S-CIELAB filters an image before CIELAB, so wood
  grain a little out of place is not a colour error (the blur 2 % of the object's height, inside the object only, a
  3-pixel edge left out); the object's own shift taken out (the picture is lit and the model's colours are not, and
  one view may be a little lighter all over); then the largest joined place more than 30 delta E unlike the picture,
  as a share of the object as seen. Without the shift taken out, the false fails were more (shown, not kept).
* **Labelled by eye:** every object it would flag at any limit that catches a teapot (pictures 12 to 14), from its
  turntable and its difference map. Eight finished objects have flawed paint by eye: the two teapots, the two
  bottles, the two buckets, the 16 GB card's flower pot, and the first trial's candle (brown smudges on its wax).
  Copies of the same object in several trial folders are counted each time in the table.

| Limit (largest unlike place) | Flagged | Flawed caught | False fails (of them passing every check) | Missed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4.7 % (catches both teapots' level) | 19 | 3: one teapot, a bucket, the candle | 16 (14) | 5 |
| 10 % | 9 | 0 | 9 (8) | 8 |

**The rule fails.** No limit catches a teapot without failing 14 objects that every trial rightly passed: barrels, a
three-legged stool, a stone birdbath, a lantern, a round table (picture 13). Their unlike places are real but not
faults: the side of a barrel lit in the picture and painted by a darker view, the rim of a table top and of a
birdbath's bowl a little out of place between the picture and the shape. And it cannot see what the picture does not:
the teapot's black side shows in the picture only as a thin band at the edge (4.7 % and 5.6 % of the object as seen),
and the 16 GB card's bottle's dark side not at all. The picture sees one side; the faults are on the others.

**What does catch the fault: the views against each other.** The teapot's and the bottle's fault is one view out of
family, and its neighbours see a strip of the same surface (round 45 degrees). `tools/trial/view_agreement.py`
compares them there: for each pair of neighbouring side views, the share of the strip where their colours differ by
more than 30; a view's disagreement is the lesser of its two pairs'. Measured on the 167 objects with their paint data
(the same labels; 12 drawn at random from those it passes, all fine):

| Limit (a view unlike both neighbours over) | Flagged | Flawed caught | False fails | Missed |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.85 | 2 | 2: both teapots (0.91, 0.90) | 0 | the bottles, and the flawed paint that is not one view's |
| 0.80 | 4 | 3: both teapots, the 16 GB bottle (0.81) | 1: a copper cooking pot (0.82; silvery patches on its copper, doubtful) | the 12 GB bottle (0.52) |
| 0.50 | 17 | 4: teapots and bottles | 13: metals (a copper kettle 0.77 and 0.75, a steel longsword, a spear head, a pickaxe, a shield's iron boss) | |

Polished metal is the limit's enemy: a view painter paints a reflection, and two views of copper or steel can differ
as much as white and black. The flower pot, the buckets and the candle are not one view's fault and are not this
measure's to catch (the pot and one bucket fail the coverage or the fill already).

## The fix for the view out of family: paint it again from another seed

The view painter draws its noise from a seed (0 for every job). Painted again from seeds 1 and 2, the six views only,
on the 12 GB card (`2026-11-11-030-reseed`, pictures 18 and 19), measured by the view agreement:

| Object | Seed 0 (as made): the most unlike view | Seed 1 | Seed 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| ceramic teapot | 0.91 (the black view) | 0.03, white all round | 0.02, white all round |
| glass bottle | 0.81 (the dark view) | 0.02, pale all round | 0.10: two neighbouring views dark |
| copper kettle | 0.77 (a silvery back view) | 0.47 | 0.51; copper all round, its front and fourth views still unlike (0.78, 0.81: the reflections) |
| wooden armchair (fine) | 0.01 | 0.01 | 0.02 |

So the fault is the seed's, not the object's: another seed paints the teapot and the bottle right. The established
pattern is the one the touch-up already follows (several tries, each judged, the best kept), and the judging is the
photo-consistency idea of texturing from photographs, applied between the views. The proposal for 0.3.0:

* paint the six views; measure each side view against its two neighbours where both see the surface;
* when a view is unlike both its neighbours over a share of 0.5 or more (17 of the 167 objects measured, 10 %, among
  them every single-view fault found), paint the views again from the next seed, up to two more times, and keep the
  try whose most unlike view is least unlike;
* record each try's measure in the job's record, and show it in the report.

A wrongly triggered repaint costs time (about 230 s on the 16 GB card), not quality, since the try kept is the most
consistent one. Two known limits, both seen here: two neighbouring views wrong together agree with each other and are
not found (the bottle's seed 2; a two-against-two split needs a third voter, and the picture is the natural one); and
polished metal's views differ by their reflections whatever the seed. Since made, with one view per place in the bake
(below): docs/objects.md, "The painted views judged against each other, and one view per place".

The bake's stipple where two views overlap (picture 04) is a separate, smaller fix: one view per place
(`tools/trial/view_selection.py`). Tried first against all six directions, it lowered the coverage (the teapot 97.8 % to
97.3 %, the armchair 91.5 % to 90.8 %, near its 90 % limit); as made, a view's paint is left out only where another
view did paint the place more squarely, and the coverage is unchanged (picture 23).

  (`aoi_cos_valid_threshold`, `uv_exp_blend_alpha`), `uv.py` (`ExponentialBlend`: weight = cos^alpha per view),
  `pipelines/pipeline_texture.py` (alpha 3, threshold 0.2, six views weighted alike).
* M. Waechter, N. Moehrle, M. Goesele, "Let There Be Color! Large-Scale Texturing of 3D Reconstructions", ECCV 2014,
  and its code (mvs-texturing, BSD 3-clause): one view chosen per face, and a photo-consistency check that drops a
  view whose colour is an outlier among the views of a face (`calculate_data_costs.cpp`: a Gaussian fitted to the
  views' mean colours in YCbCr, at least 4 views, up to 10 rounds).
* V. Lempitsky, D. Ivanov, "Seamless Mosaicing of Image-Based Texture Maps", CVPR 2007: a view per face, the seams
  levelled.
* X. Zhang, B. Wandell, "A spatial extension of CIELAB for digital color image reproduction" (S-CIELAB), SID 1996 and
  J. SID 5(1) 1997: colours compared after a spatial filter, so fine texture does not count as a colour error.
* Y. Zeng et al., "Paint3D: Paint Anything 3D with Lighting-Less Texture Diffusion Models", CVPR 2024.
* Black Forest Labs' prompting guide ("Building a good prompt", docs.bfl.ai): describe the visible details that set
  a subject apart rather than relying on its name, and put first what matters most.

## A describer for any name: the trial (11 November 2026)

The words table fixes four names; a new prompt brings new names the picture model knows only loosely. Could a small
local language model write the description for any name? A time-boxed trial, pictures only.

**The model.** Qwen3.5-9B (Apache-2.0 by its model card and its licence file, no use restrictions stated), quantised
to 4 bits (5.5 GB), run by llama.cpp (MIT) on the CPU, its thinking off, temperature 0, one example in the words' own
style (`tools/trial/describe_things.py`). Qwen3.5-4B was tried first: its descriptions were wrong more often (a
pillory "with four arms ending in rings", a water wheel "on a vertical axle", a club "a long stick"), so the 9B was
measured. The descriptions it gave are kept with the trial (`2026-11-11-030-describer/described9-heldout.json`).

**Measured** (`2026-11-11-030-describer`, the 16 GB card): twelve names not in any run before (a butter churn, a sundial,
an hourglass, an astrolabe, a quern stone, a fireplace bellows, an ox yoke, a wooden pillory, a trebuchet, a weaving
loom, a cider press, a mead horn) and four well-known controls (a barrel, a chair, a clay jug, an anvil), eight tries
each with the plain words and eight with the description added after the user's words (as `THING_PICTURE_WORDS` adds
it). All 256 tries labelled by eye from blind sheets, one a name, the two wordings shuffled under codes, before the key
was opened (picture 27):

| | Plain words | Described |
|---|---|---|
| the twelve names: tries that are plainly the thing | 39 of 96 | **54 of 96** |
| the four controls | 30 of 32 | 32 of 32 |

By name, plain against described: a butter churn 0 against 8, a sundial 0 against 7, a pillory 0 against 5, a quern
stone 0 against 4, a trebuchet 4 against 7; the hourglass, loom and cider press 8, 8 and 8 against 8, 8 and 7; a
fireplace bellows and an ox yoke 0 either way (the picture model paints a sack and a bull's horns whatever it is told);
and two names lost: a mead horn 8 against 0 (the model described "a hollow conical bowl ... with a long spout") and an
astrolabe 3 against 0. A wrong description destroys a name the picture model already knew; and three of the anvil's
described tries have their horn curled upward, as the description said.

**The judge as a safety net.** SigLIP 2 passed 148 of the 155 right tries but also 37 of the 86 wrong ones (it does not
know a yoke or a bellows either). But it refused all eight described mead horns and passed five plain ones. So paint
both wordings and let the judge choose: simulated on these tries (the try with the highest share among those that pass;
ties shared), a right picture is chosen for 4.8 of the 12 names with the plain words, 7.8 with the descriptions, 8.7
with both (the controls 3.75, 4.0 and 3.9 of 4).

**What it would cost.** A description is one short answer from the 9B (twenty took a few minutes on 12 CPU threads; not
timed one by one); painting both wordings doubles the picture stage's tries (about 80 s an object on the 16 GB card), or
halves each wording's.

**Limits.** Twelve names, one set of seeds, one labeller; the choice by the judge is simulated, not run through the
pipeline; the 9B's knowledge is the ceiling (a bellows, a yoke), and its descriptions name materials the user did not
ask for. Not made: next would be the pipeline painting half its tries from each wording, measured on new held-out names
end to end.
