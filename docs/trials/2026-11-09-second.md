the longbow made again, unpainted.

## (a) Does a floor shadow harm the model? No; the check now judges the cut-out

The picture model puts a floor shadow under nearly everything: "no cast shadow" failed 32 to 34 of 40
tries with every lead. Measured:

* By eye, the cut-outs of the 76 pictures that failed only on the shadow (and floor) all followed the
  object and left the shadow out; close-ups of the base of the worst-scored ones showed the same.
* The models made from shadowed pictures (the book, the chest, the shield, the jug) have no dark slab
  and no dark foot, and the book passed every check.
* A first measure of shadow inside the cut-out by colour (dark, colourless pixels in its foot) flagged
  12 pictures, every one wrongly: the object's own grey or dark foot (a sword's point, an iron hoop,
  black table legs, a white table top). A second, by the edge alone (no colour step where the cut-out's
  edge meets a shadow), flagged dark feet on their own contact shadows. The measure kept joins both:
  backdrop-coloured pixels inside the cut-out's foot, joined to a stretch of its edge that runs through
  a shadow with no step. On all 212 pictures it flags 2, both a white table top as pale and grey as the
  backdrop, which it cannot tell from a shadow; those fail safe (another try is painted).

So the check is "no shadow or floor in the cut-out" (at most 0.5 % of it), and the shadow and floor line
on the backdrop are shown, not counted. "Plain backdrop" counts only what is more colourful than the
backdrop: its own grey in a shadow, on a floor or in a vignette's corners had failed clean pictures (a
grey thing beside the object is no longer counted there; "one piece" finds it if it is cut out with
the object). Picture tries passing: 38, 36 and 39 of 40 for the three leads (5, 6 and 5 before), and
74 of 76 in the first trial; what still fails is a second piece in the cut-out. The lead stays
"floating".

## (b) The chair's and table's outlines: the measure misread them

By eye both match their pictures closely: the outline pictures are green but for thin slivers along
legs and spindles. The overlap counts a leg a pixel or two off wholly against it (the chair 79 %, the
table 83 %). The outline is now judged by its edges' distance, as a share of the height: at most 1.9 %
on average (a compact shape offset all round by that overlaps its picture 85 %, the old limit) and 4 % at
the 95th percentile (a part out of place). The chair: 0.44 % and 1.07 %; the table: 0.73 % and 2.08 %.
The first trial's table, with its web between the legs, still fails: 1.34 % and 5.47 %. The overlap is
shown beside it. Both now pass every check, unpainted.

The chair also stood right with all four shape seeds (its back legs no longer short), though the
picture model still painted it from near eye level: the words "from about thirty degrees above" were
not followed. They stay; the regression set watches the chair.

## (c) The longbow: not made in release 1

Asked to face the viewer, the picture model painted the bow in profile with its string; the shape
model filled the space between bow and string into a flat leaf-shaped plate with holes (kink 10.6 to
28.5°, limbs mirroring 34 to 41 % off). With the first trial's wave, that is two different failures
from two views. A bow prompt is now answered: "Bows are not made yet: the picture model does not paint
a bow from the side that shows its curve, and the shape model fills the space between a bow and its
string. They come in a later release." The string drawn by code stays ready for when they come.

## (d) The regression set

`tools/trial/regression.toml`: thirteen cases, each a model a check caught or once misread, with the
verdicts it must keep; `tools/trial/regression.py run DATA` judges them on the CPU in about three
minutes (all 13 hold; the data is not in this repository: docs/objects.md, "The regression set").
`regression-prompts.txt` makes them again on the card before a release; `unseen-final.txt` holds
prompts kept aside during development and made once for the release's check.
